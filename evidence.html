What the evidence supports

A working system can show that an approach is possible. Establishing when it improves valuable outcomes takes a different kind of evidence.

What people have built

OpenAI's Harness engineering describes a team building a new product with agent-written code. The account explains how isolated application instances, inspectable behavior and enforced architectural constraints helped agents complete more of the work. It gives us concrete arrangements to examine. Its estimated saving against writing the product by hand is not a controlled comparison of organizational effectiveness.

StrongDM's software factory account describes development driven by specifications, behavioral scenarios and replicas of external services. It suggests a different place to invest engineering effort: constructing an environment in which proposed behavior can be assessed repeatedly. It remains a first-party account. A replica can miss a real service's behavior, and a separate scenario set can still encode a mistaken requirement.

These accounts inform our discussion of factories. They do not establish that the same arrangement will pay for itself in an existing product, or that every team should stop reading code. A useful adaptation would name the class of work, the required evidence and the decisions people retain, then examine the cost of operating it.

Be clear about what improved

METR's February 2026 update explains why its follow-up developer productivity experiment could not give a reliable estimate of current effects. Participation and task selection had changed; concurrent agents complicated the recording of time. This matters when we compare workflows that organize work differently. An apparent improvement can partly reflect which work enters the comparison.

Its later note, Task Substitution and Uplift, separates gains on the old task mix, gains on the new task mix and gains in value. This is a theoretical model with stated assumptions. It helps frame a practical question: if a farm runs investigations nobody would previously have commissioned, what decisions improve because of them? Their hypothetical manual cost is not a saving we have actually made.

DORA's 2025 report emphasizes the organizational conditions surrounding AI adoption. Its survey findings support examining the wider system; they do not prove that a particular practice causes a fixed productivity gain.

For a trial, keep the useful outcome in view. Record elapsed time, human attention, machine and operating costs, failures, rework and any effort shifted to customers or other teams. Include work that remains outside the new route. When the nature of the outcome changes, explain that change instead of forcing unlike results into one ratio.

Check what people learned

Anthropic's study of coding skill formation examined 52 mostly junior developers learning an unfamiliar Python library. The group with AI assistance scored lower on an immediate comprehension quiz; the difference in completion speed was not statistically significant. This short study does not tell us what happens to expertise over years. It does show why completing a task is insufficient evidence of understanding.

Our proposed response is to give understanding its own checks. Can an engineer predict a failure they have not seen, explain a boundary or recognize when a rule should change? That is an investigation to run alongside increased delegation, rather than something to assume will follow from it.

When independent actions remain acceptable together

Bailis and colleagues' Coordination Avoidance in Database Systems develops a formal criterion for avoiding coordination while preserving invariants in its database model. Balegas and colleagues' work on numeric invariants describes distributing rights to consume bounded resources. These are technical results under explicit assumptions, not studies of software engineering organizations.

Our synthetic comparison illustrates fixed and transferable rights against a pooled baseline. It counts modeled requests and unmet needs, and shows failure under skew and coordination unavailability. It does not measure human effort, economic return, reliability in production or compounding. The research note (review draft) records the assumptions, results and counterexamples.

The organizational hypothesis is that investment in observable obligations, boundaries and appropriately scoped authority may remove recurring coordination at comparable outcome and risk. Test against capable automated coordination, include upkeep and missed demand, and observe what happens when conditions change. The proposed experiment has no reported field results.

How to read the claims here

A research finding belongs to its study population, tasks and conditions. A documented account tells us what its authors report doing and observing. An illustrative example lets us examine a proposed design without claiming it happened. A hypothesis needs an observation that could count against it.

The customer integration story is illustrative. The billing example demonstrates behavior in a small synthetic model. The claim that one capability can make later capabilities easier to develop is a hypothesis to investigate beyond repeated use of the first tool. Each kind of material serves a purpose, provided we do not silently turn it into another.

Our open questions set out the next investigations. For a useful account from your own organization, describe the starting constraint, what changed, the costs you included and what happened later. A case where the idea failed would help us revise it.
