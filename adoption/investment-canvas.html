Enough detail to make a decision

Use this for a bet that needs a planning conversation. A small fix inside an ordinary task can stay small. The questions are prompts, so leave out what you do not need and copy the note into the issue you already use.

Your entries stay in this page and are not sent anywhere. Copy them before leaving; this worksheet does not save a draft.

Read a worked example

This hypothetical billing team wants to judge routine database migrations without waiting for another team's specialist. Its existing lock traces, synthetic traffic cases and database sandbox may be enough to build a useful check.

The team agrees a two-day build cap, half a day for a receiving engineer to try it and a review after three eligible migrations or twelve weeks. It will compare the receiver's judgment with the specialist's under the same acceptance standard, including cases the new check rejects or cannot judge.

The cases might later help build a recovery rehearsal. That is a separate hypothesis. If the next release still waits for a downtime decision, the team has learned where to take the problem next.

The proposed investment

Name the person or team, the useful work and the conditions it covers.

Use a recent case. Include another plausible explanation and the option of removing the chore.

Include people's knowledge, decisions and access as well as tools. What would need adapting?

Choose one task that can show whether the investment is worth pursuing.

What is its main purpose?

State the prediction, the quality bar and what you will observe. Count failed attempts and work shifted to others.

Name what that capability could inherit. Leave this blank if the immediate benefit is enough.

Name the owner, receiver and decision authority. Include build, adoption, review, upkeep and the work displaced.

Set a review trigger and a date in case the expected work never arrives. Say what would justify stopping.

Come back after someone uses it

Add what happened to the same issue. Could the receiver make the decision? How much help did they need? Did the work move forward, or merely wait somewhere else? Then decide whether to keep, simplify, repair or retire the investment.

If a later capability is built, record what it reused and the new work required. That is where the compounding claim can be tested. A useful immediate return needs no larger story.
